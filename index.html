FPGA-based Stereo Camera

An FPGA-based stereo vision system uses a field-programmable gate array to process synchronized images from two cameras and generate real-time depth information. Through hardware pipelining and parallel processing, the system can perform image preprocessing, stereo rectification, feature or disparity matching, and 3D depth-map generation with extremely low latency.

  • Massive Hardware Parallelism: Multiple pixels and image-processing operations can be handled simultaneously during each clock cycle, enabling high-throughput execution across preprocessing, rectification, stereo matching, and depth-estimation stages.
  • Ultra-Low Latency: Dedicated hardware processing avoids the scheduling and software overhead associated with CPUs and GPUs, making the architecture suitable for time-critical control and perception applications.
  • High Energy Efficiency: The system can deliver high frame rates while operating within a power budget below approximately 10 W, providing strong performance per watt compared with many CPU- or GPU-based alternatives.
  • Compact Embedded Implementation: The low power and reduced cooling requirements enable integration into compact, rugged, and remotely deployed enclosures without the need for large power supplies or complex thermal-management systems.
